All four entries were submitted to MICCAI 2026 (Strasbourg, France) as an independent researcher.

### AIMS-TBI 2026
* **Task:** detect lesion presence per subject, and segment lesions, in moderate-to-severe traumatic brain injury on T1-weighted MRI.
* **Result:** 8th of 22 entries on the final detection leaderboard; 11th of 17 on segmentation (official ranking announced 1 October 2026). Final-phase scores: detection balanced accuracy 0.8457, sensitivity 0.8625, specificity 0.8289; segmentation lesion-containing Dice 0.5048, HD95 30.7638 mm, ASSD (average symmetric surface distance) 11.1496 mm.
* **Paper:** "Diagnosing the Recall Tail: A Class-Imbalance Study of nnU-Net for Detection and Segmentation of Heterogeneous Moderate–Severe TBI Lesions", sole author. Accepted; camera-ready submitted. The paper link is not public yet.
* **Method:** a self-configuring nnU-Net, ResEnc-M preset, 3D, five-fold ensemble (patch 128 × 160 × 112, batch size 2, Dice plus cross-entropy). A single 16 GB consumer GPU fixed ResEnc-M as the largest usable preset. Each fold trained 250 epochs with the learning-rate schedule compressed to anneal fully by epoch 250 (about 39 hours in total, against about 150 hours for the default 1,000 epochs). One model serves both tasks: segmentation keeps 26-connected components of at least 13 voxels (never only the largest, because 83% of lesion-bearing subjects are multifocal); detection calls a lesion present when surviving volume above the 10-voxel scoring floor totals at least τ = 100 voxels. τ was fixed on out-of-fold predictions (balanced accuracy 0.859) and never tuned later; the held-out optimum (τ = 50, balanced accuracy 0.950) was deliberately not taken. Data: 552 training subjects (41.7% lesion-free) plus 102 held-out labelled subjects. Median inference about 35 s per case against a 7-minute limit.
* **Finding:** what looked like poor lesion boundaries was a small-lesion recall tail. Of 305 scored cross-validation cases, 22 (7.2%) failed outright (9 predicted nothing, 13 predicted in the wrong place); the other 283 had a median ASSD of 2.3 mm. Removing the 22 lowers mean ASSD from 8.55 mm to 6.19 mm while the median stays near 2.4 mm, so the failures carry about a third of the mean. The failure rate falls from 27.9% in the smallest volume quintile to zero in the largest two, and no failure occurs above the median lesion volume of 2,034 voxels. Lowering the softmax threshold and batch-pooled Dice recovered no misses. Test-time mirroring gained +0.014 Dice in cross-validation (95% CI +0.009 to +0.019) but only +0.0010 on the test set (fourteen times smaller; write it as +0.0010, with three zeros after the decimal point), where ASSD worsened by 0.34 mm.
* **Limitations:** the reduced 250-epoch schedule makes this a characterised baseline, not a ceiling; the batch-Dice result comes from a single fold; diffusion data went unused because the test set is T1-weighted only.
* **Page:** /challenges/aims-tbi-2026

### MICCAI Educational Challenge 2026
* **Result:** finalist. Submission title "Your GPU Is Lying To You", subtitle "And so is your test harness, your docstring, your validation set, and your cross-validation." Built from the AIMS-TBI container work of June and July 2026.
* **Content:** six instruments that reported success while being wrong: the GPU (`torch.cuda.is_available()` returned True while every kernel launch failed, with silent CPU fallback, so local timings of 175 to 433 s per case came from the CPU against about 35 s on the target GPU, and 433 s exceeds the 420 s limit); the test harness (four tracebacks while the container was correct on all four cases); a docstring (it promised an all-background mask on any exception, but the read that could raise sat above the `try`); the validation set (a file named for 100 cases held 103 images and 102 labels); calibration (τ = 50 promised +0.027 balanced accuracy on held-out data and scored 0.0010 lower than τ = 100 on test); and cross-validation (+0.014 Dice became +0.0010 on test, and the target metric moved the wrong way). It includes a ten-point preflight checklist and ships synthetic data, so it runs without weights, a GPU or a data agreement.
* **Finding:** six instruments reported success while being wrong, and almost none of them needed real data to catch.
* **Page:** /challenges/mec-2026 (full tutorial: /notes/gpu-is-lying; OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=68qQDKMuwi). Companion repository: https://github.com/GeofrayPaulJ/MEC.

### TopAneu 2026, Task 2
* **Task:** vessel-specific intracranial aneurysm segmentation. Team Prometheus, solo entrant.
* **Result:** 10th of 30 entries, Task 2 final test phase, mean position 10.1. Dice 0.1521 (winning entry's Dice: 0.2632), Precision 0.4367, F1 0.3110, Recall 0.3499, MCC 0.4435, VolSim 0.1699, HD95 226.7214. Task 1 is pending: the organisers populate it from the Task 2 submission and that fusion is in progress, so there is no Task 1 rank. This entry is not a publication.
* **Method:** Stage 1 detects with two nnU-Net v2 models (CTA and MRA; ResEnc-M, 3d_fullres, 500 epochs, five folds each), chosen by the image's minimum intensity, with test-time mirroring off (it changed no decision yet took 66 to 75% of runtime) and candidates of at least 1.0 mm³. Vessel context comes from the organisers' publicly released TopBrain vessel model (Zenodo record 21959166, CC-BY-4.0), which reproduced ground-truth laterality on 99.28% of instances against 52% for an in-house head. Stage 2 assigns each lesion with laterality-invariant features and a gradient-boosted 28-way delateralised site classifier, reassembled to 52 classes with the geometric side, plus a second gradient-boosted model that rejects false positives. Runtime is 218 s per CTA case against a 720 s limit, with peak host memory 10.2 GiB.
* **Finding:** detection was not the bottleneck. Most errors were lesions found correctly and then assigned to the wrong vessel site, concentrated in sites with fewer than ten training examples; more examples of rare sites would help, more detection capacity would not. Out-of-fold Task 1 MCC was 0.2040 (324 cases), and nine of the 52 classes have no support in the released training data.
* **Limitations:** Stage 1 was not retrained after the September 2026 dataset revision. That revision changed only 19 of the 389 instances, nearly all of them relabels. Stage 2 was refitted on the corrected labels.
* **Page:** /challenges/topaneu26

### RARE26
* **Task:** part of EndoVis at MICCAI 2026. Classify Barrett's oesophagus endoscopy images as neoplastic or non-dysplastic, scored by PPV@90Recall under simulated 1:100 prevalence.
* **Result:** Open Development phase, on the platform: AUROC 0.6466, PPV@90Recall 0.0113. At the time, the ten highest visible entries ranged from PPV@90Recall 0.0243 to 0.0332. No final-phase rank is given.
* **Method:** "Below the Noise Floor: A Domain-Generalised ConvNeXt Ensemble for Barrett's Neoplasia Detection" (A4-corrected): five ConvNeXt-Base members (ImageNet-1k initialised, seeds 0 to 4, 87,567,489 parameters each) fused by averaging raw logits. Inputs are 384 × 384 crops of the largest inscribed square of the circular field of view, with domain-generalisation augmentation (colour, blur, noise, compression, border randomisation) at 2 to 3 times typical magnitude. Training: centre-and-class-balanced sampler, plain binary cross-entropy, AdamW (learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.05), batch size 38, 30 epochs, bf16 with an fp32 head; no additional training data. Validation: stratified group five-fold with perceptual-hash near-duplicate grouping (pooled out-of-fold AUROC 0.9614, 95% CI 0.9410 to 0.9782), leave-one-centre-out (0.9363 and 0.9611) and an external cohort (0.972).
* **Finding:** Both internal estimates overshot the platform result (PPV@90Recall 0.0113): pooled out-of-fold by about sevenfold, leave-one-centre-out by two- to threefold. Pooled cross-validation was the more misleading estimate under the shift between centres. The pooled out-of-fold projection (computed in July) was 0.0782; the leave-one-centre-out projections were 0.0252 and 0.0333. Any answer about RARE26's estimates says that both overshot the platform result (0.0113). Never claim the estimates predicted the field's range.
* **Challenge paper:** Co-author, as a participating team and at the organisers' invitation, of the organisers' journal article summarising the RARE26 challenge (in preparation).
* **Limitations:** the twelve-centre platform AUROC (0.6466) is 0.31 below the pooled estimate (0.9614), and the cause is unresolved after ten tested hypotheses. A separate limitation, unrelated to the leaderboard projection: when choosing ensemble components during six weeks of screening, the statistic used to accept a component was uncalibrated, so most component-selection decisions cannot be distinguished from noise at this sample size. It played no part in the July projections.
* **Page:** /challenges/rare26 (code: https://github.com/GeofrayPaulJ/rare26-submission)
