Research interests

Failure modes of medical image analysis models and how to measure them: small-lesion recall under class imbalance, validation optimism under centre and prevalence shift, rare-class label assignment, and annotation quality as a ceiling on measured performance. Applied in computational radiology, chiefly neuroimaging (traumatic brain injury, intracranial aneurysms on CTA and MRA, post-mortem fetal brain MRI); in computational pathology (whole-slide images of brain tissue); and in endoscopy.

Challenge research: MICCAI 2026, solo entries

AIMS-TBI 2026. Lesion detection and segmentation in moderate–severe traumatic brain injury, T1-weighted MRI.

Result. 8th of 22 entries on the final detection leaderboard (balanced accuracy 0.846); 11th of 17 on segmentation (lesion Dice 0.505) (official ranking announced 1 October 2026).

Method. Self-configuring nnU-Net (ResEnc-M, 3D, five-fold ensemble) trained on 552 subjects. One model serves both tasks; the detection threshold was fixed on out-of-fold predictions and never tuned on later data.

Finding. Much of the apparent boundary error was a small-lesion recall tail: 22 of 305 cases (7.2%) failed outright, none above the median lesion volume, and excluding them lowered mean ASSD from 8.55 to 6.19 mm. Threshold lowering, batch Dice and test-time mirroring left the tail intact. Paper [1].

MICCAI Educational Challenge 2026. Finalist.

Tutorial [2]. Built from the AIMS-TBI container work: six instruments that reported success while being wrong (a CUDA availability check masking a silent CPU fallback, a test harness, a docstring, a validation set, a calibrated threshold, cross-validation), each with its mechanism and the rule that catches it, plus a ten-point preflight checklist that runs on synthetic data.

TopAneu 2026, Task 2. Vessel-specific intracranial aneurysm segmentation, CTA and MRA.

Result. 10th of 30 entries, Task 2 final test phase (mean position 10.1 across seven metrics); Dice 0.1521 against the winning entry’s 0.2632. Task 1 result pending.

Method. nnU-Net v2 detection with separate CTA and MRA models, then gradient-boosted assignment of each aneurysm to one of 52 vessel sites from laterality-invariant geometric features, with vessel context from the organisers’ public TopBrain model and a second classifier rejecting false positives; 218 s per CTA case (limit 720 s).

Finding. Detection was not the bottleneck. Most errors were correct detections assigned to the wrong vessel, concentrated in sites with fewer than ten training examples; nine of the 52 classes had none.

RARE26 (EndoVis). Barrett’s oesophagus neoplasia classification at 1:100 prevalence, endoscopy.

Result. Open Development phase: AUROC 0.6466, PPV@90Recall 0.0113. Co-author of the challenge overview paper [4].

Method. Five-member ConvNeXt-Base ensemble with augmentation at two to three times typical magnitude against acquisition-centre shortcuts; near-duplicate-grouped and leave-one-centre-out cross-validation; a 100-image external cohort held for evaluation only.

Finding. Both internal estimates overshot the platform result (PPV@90Recall 0.0113): pooled out-of-fold by about sevenfold, leave-one-centre-out by two- to threefold. Pooled cross-validation was the more misleading estimate under the shift between centres. The cause of the gap remains unresolved after ten tested hypotheses. Report [3].
